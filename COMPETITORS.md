# Competitor landing-page teardown

Scanned **12 August 2026**. Three foreign mobile-first CMMS vendors, the bracket
MainteNeat competes with directly on product shape:

| Vendor | URL |
|---|---|
| MaintainX | https://www.getmaintainx.com/ |
| Limble | https://limble.com/ |
| UpKeep | https://upkeep.com/ |

**How to read this.** Everything below is what these vendors *say about
themselves* on their own homepage. None of it is verified, and none of it is
endorsed. Claims are attributed on purpose — "MaintainX says 14,000 companies"
is a fact about their marketing, not about the world. Only the homepage was
scanned; pricing and feature pages deeper in each site may say more.

## 2. What all three do identically

This is the table-stakes list. Every one of these appears on all three
homepages, and **not one of the three argues for any of them** — they are listed
in a features menu or a grid below the fold, never in a headline:

- Work order management
- Preventive maintenance
- Predictive maintenance
- Asset / equipment management
- Parts & inventory management
- Reporting, analytics, dashboards
- A technician mobile app
- Integrations with ERP and other systems
- Resource / labour planning
- A long industry list (manufacturing, food & beverage, facilities, fleet,
  healthcare, energy, hospitality…)
- **AI, prominently** — MaintainX "Industrial AI built for the frontline",
  Limble "Practical AI", UpKeep "Nova AI"

Structural habits they also share:

- **Logo wall immediately below the fold.** All three lead their proof with
  recognisable enterprise names.
- **Case studies with a hard number in the title**, not a paragraph of praise.
- **Review-site badges** (G2, Capterra) treated as third-party validation.
- **Two CTAs, always** — one low-commitment, one sales-led.
- Nobody sells the module list. They sell a *position*, then use the modules as
  evidence.

> Confirms the instinct behind this project: work orders and inventory are the
> price of entry in this category. Leading with them signals you have nothing
> else.

## 3. Vendor profiles

### MaintainX — the authority play

**Position:** we are the biggest and the most credible, and we are the AI one.

Headline is a category statement, not a benefit: *"Intelligent maintenance and
asset management."* The second section is literally titled **"The clear industry
leader."** Third is **"Industrial AI built for the frontline."**

**Named customers:** Carrier, Norfolk Southern, Cintas, Michaels, US LBM,
Univar Solutions, Hudsonville, Redimix.

**Quantified case studies:**
- Cintas — "200 sites implemented in 9 weeks"
- Michaels — "70% less mean time to repair"
- Redimix — "53% lower maintenance costs"
- US LBM — "40+ plants standardized maintenance"

**Proof stack (heaviest of the three by a wide margin):**
- 14,000 companies
- SOC 2 Type 2, ISO/IEC 27001:2022, GDPR, SAP Silver Partner
- 2025 Forbes Cloud 100, Deloitte 2025
- G2 Best Software 2026, Capterra Best Ease of Use 2026, plus four
  category-specific G2 badges (Best Usability, Most Implementable, Best
  Estimated ROI, Users Most Likely to Recommend)

**Modules named:** CMMS, EAM, work orders, preventive, predictive, asset
management, parts inventory, checklists & inspections, facility maintenance,
resource planning, vendor management, reporting, industrial controls & IoT, work
requests, AI operations, integrations marketplace, expert implementation.

**Selling points, in their order:** market leadership → AI → enterprise
readiness and security → award wall. Notice how late the features come.

**What they are really selling:** *safety of choice.* This page is built to make
a committee comfortable. Every element answers "will I be criticised for picking
this?"

---

### Limble — the outcome play

**Position:** we make production better, and people actually like using us.

The only headline of the three that names a **business outcome instead of a
software category**: *"Optimize preventive maintenance for peak production."*
The sub is all verbs and consequences — *"Slash downtime, simplify work orders…
so operations never miss a beat."*

**Named customers:** Unilever, Sulzer, McDonald's, General Mills, Aspen
Surgical, Barnard. Case studies span Powder Mountain, Renfro Foods, J.J. Taylor,
Caglia, Bentek, Island Abbey Nutritionals — deliberately mid-market and varied,
not just enterprise.

**Proof — and this is their most distinctive move:** alongside "Rated #1 by
50,000+ maintenance professionals" (4.8 from 681 ratings), they publish **live
platform usage counters**:
- 16,548,218+ tasks completed
- 750,256+ work requests submitted
- 9,235,587+ parts tracked
- 8,568,785+ dashboard widgets created

That is proof of *adoption*, not proof of *purchase* — it says the software is
being used every day, which is a different and harder claim than a customer
count. It also quietly argues the thing MainteNeat argues.

They also state the stake numerically: **"Unplanned downtime costs 10x more than
preventive maintenance."**

**Sections in order:** Who it's for → Smarter maintenance, smoother operations →
Work orders, simplified → Prevent downtime before it costs you → See the big
picture → Spend smarter → **Proven results, measurable impact** → real-time
collaboration → **Start seeing results quickly** → "Empowering the humans who
keep the world's assets running."

**Implementation is a selling point, not fine print:** "Seamless
implementation", "Implementation your way", 24/7 support, dedicated success
team, training and resources.

**Pricing:** no figures, but the secondary CTA is **"calculate my price"** — a
gated calculator. Reads as transparent without publishing a number.

**What they are really selling:** *low friction and fast payback.* Closest of
the three to MainteNeat's own thesis — and the most direct threat to it
rhetorically.

### UpKeep — the breadth play

**Position:** don't buy a CMMS, buy the platform that replaces four tools.

Headline is bluntly SEO-shaped — *"CMMS & Maintenance Management Software"* —
with the real positioning in the sub: **"One Platform for CMMS, Safety, and
Asset Operations."**

**Product suite (the actual differentiator):** Maintenance Management,
Enterprise Asset Management, **UpKeep Safety** (EHS), **UpKeep Learn** (LMS),
**UpKeep Fleet**, UpKeep Edge, UpKeep Intelligence, UpKeep Studio, **Nova AI**.
They are the only one of the three selling adjacent categories.

**Named customers:** Unilever, PepsiCo, Chevron, Caterpillar, Yamaha, Stratasys,
McDonald's, Raceway, Marriott, Aramark.

**Case study numbers:** McDonald's — 50% downtime reduction, repair time "dropped
from 3–4 days to just one." Raceway positioned to "grow to 200 locations."

**Proof:** 4,000+ companies; 2,000+ five-star reviews across G2, Capterra and
the Apple App Store; "Rated a Leader in CMMS." No security or compliance badges
on the homepage.

**Pricing — the only vendor of the three to publish it on-site:**

| Tier | Price | Nova AI credits |
|---|---|---|
| Essential | **$24 / user / month** | 250 / mo |
| Premium *(Most Popular)* | **$55 / user / month** | 500 / mo |
| Professional | Request a quote | 750 / mo |
| Enterprise | Request a quote | 1,000 / mo |

Note the AI-credit metering — AI is being packaged as a consumable upsell, not a
free capability.

**Also claims:** mobile-first, **offline mobile mode**, ERP and sensor
integrations, free trial with no credit card.

**What they are really selling:** *consolidation.* The pitch is fewer vendors,
one login, one bill.

## 4. What they disclose — and what they hide

**Disclosed freely by all three:**
- Customer names, at enterprise scale
- Outcome percentages attached to a named customer
- Review-site ratings and award badges
- The complete module list
- Industries served

**Withheld or gated:**

| Information | MaintainX | Limble | UpKeep |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price | Hidden | Calculator | **Published** |
| Contract length / minimums | Not stated | Not stated | Not stated |
| Implementation fees | Not stated | Not stated | Not stated |
| Data residency / hosting location | Not stated | Not stated | Not stated |
| Support hours & channels | Not stated | **24/7 stated** | Not stated |
| Security posture | **Badged prominently** | Absent | Absent |

Two patterns worth taking:

1. **Pricing opacity is the norm, and it is a vulnerability.** Two of three make
   a buyer talk to sales to learn a number. Publishing a real figure is a
   differentiator in this category, not a weakness.
2. **Nobody says where the data lives.** Not one of the three states a hosting
   country or region on their homepage. MaintainX offers GDPR compliance, which
   is a legal posture, not residency.

## 6. What this means for MainteNeat

### Claims that are NOT differentiators (all three already make them)

- Mobile-first, technician-friendly interface
- Fast onboarding / quick time to value — **Limble sells this hard**
- Predictive maintenance and AI — **all three present these as shipping today**
- Work orders, preventive plans, inventory, reporting
- Serving industrial SMEs alongside enterprises

The AI point deserves attention. The decision to state honestly that predictive
maintenance is not shipped is defensible and rare, but understand the cost: in
this bracket, AI is not a future promise, it is present-tense homepage copy.
That gap will be noticed by anyone comparing tabs.

### Open ground — nothing in this scan contests these

1. **Data residency in Morocco.** No competitor states a hosting country at all.
2. **Invoicing in dirhams.** All published pricing here is USD, per seat.
3. **Flat price per company.** Every disclosed model is per user, per month —
   $24–$55 at UpKeep. A plant with 20 users is looking at roughly
   $480–$1,100/month before quotes. MainteNeat's 1 000 MAD flat is roughly an
   order of magnitude apart, and it does not punish hiring.
4. **Support that physically reaches the plant.** Limble claims 24/7; none
   claims presence on site, and none can claim it in Morocco.
5. **QR reporting with no account at all.** All three have work-request intake,
   but none of them leads with anonymous, login-free reporting from the machine
   as a headline claim. This is the sharpest product-level difference available.
6. **A published entry price.** Beating two of three on transparency costs
   nothing.

### Weak spots to consider

- **No free entry path.** MaintainX offers a free plan, UpKeep a free trial with
  no credit card. MainteNeat offers neither — the only routes are a demo and a
  contact form. That is a higher first step than two of three competitors ask
  for.
- **No proof layer, of any kind.** Every vendor here leads with logos, counts
  and outcome percentages. With no customers yet, that section cannot be
  honestly written — but its absence is conspicuous against these pages, and it
  is the strongest argument for landing the first reference customers quickly
  and getting permission to name them.
- **No security or compliance story.** MaintainX makes SOC 2 and ISO 27001 a
  headline. For an industrial buyer trusting a vendor with plant data,
  "sovereign hosting" is a good answer only until someone asks what certifies
  it.

### The one-line read

> They compete on scale, outcomes and breadth. None of them competes on
> proximity — currency, jurisdiction, or a person who can stand in front of the
> machine. That is the ground nobody in this scan is standing on.
